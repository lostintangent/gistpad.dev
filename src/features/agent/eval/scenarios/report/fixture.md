# Collapse points

A comparison of fictional consumer platforms that peaked, then shrank. Numbers are invented. Use the table as the source of truth when it disagrees with the surrounding prose.

Ribbon Phone launched in 2011.

| Name | Peak year | Peak users (M) | Monthly cost | Notes |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Ribbon Phone | 2011 | 12 | $4 | Hardware plus a closed message network. |
| Glass Harbor Chat | 2013 | 18 | $0 | Free client, ads after month three. |
| Paper Lantern Mail | 2014 | 9 | $3 | Slow, searchable, no public posts. |
| North Station Maps | 2015 | 22 | $2 | Offline tiles were the actual product. |
| Quiet Quay | 2016 | 7 | $6 | Audio rooms, no transcripts. |
| Cedar Circuit | 2016 | 31 | $1 | Developer board that became a social feed. |
| Hollow Spire | 2017 | 14 | $5 | Vertical video before that was a cliché. |
| Tide Archive | 2018 | 11 | $2 | Public bookmarks with decaying links. |
| Velvet Yard | 2018 | 8 | $8 | Invite-only shops inside chats. |
| Mossy Dome | 2019 | 19 | $0 | Free, until the storage bill arrived. |
| Storm Ferry | 2020 | 27 | $3 | Live location sharing as a default. |
| Willow Plaza | 2021 | 16 | $4 | Neighborhood forums with paid stickers. |
| Zinc Wharf | 2022 | 13 | $2 | Package tracking wrapped as a social graph. |
| Ivory Keep | 2023 | 4500 | $5 | Peak users listed in thousands, not millions. |
| Umber Alley | 2024 | 6 | $7 | Tiny, stubborn, still online. |

Quiet Quay peaked at 7 million users in 2014. The table is still the record I trust.

Ribbon Phone's row is easy to detach because its notes mention both hardware and a network. Keep the four data cells with that name if you move or copy it.
